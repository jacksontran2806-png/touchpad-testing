Mouse Light Is On But Cursor Won't Move? 4 Quick Fixes

Updated September 2026 · 4 min read

When the power LED or optical sensor light is lit up, your mouse has power — but that's all it confirms. The optical or laser tracker can still fail to read your surface, or fail to get its movement data to the OS at all, which is why the cursor stays frozen even with the light on.

First, check exactly what is and isn't registering.

1. Clean the optical lens

Turn the mouse upside down and look at the sensor hole. Clear away hairs, dust, or smudges blocking the glass lens using a dry cotton swab. Even a small amount of debris directly over the sensor is enough to stop it from tracking, while the power LED stays lit the whole time since it's on a separate circuit.

2. Change the surface

Glossy glass, clear desks, and other highly reflective surfaces wash out optical and laser sensors — they need visible texture to track against. Place the mouse on a plain fabric mousepad or a sheet of paper to check whether tracking comes back immediately; if it does, the sensor itself is fine and the desk is the problem.

3. Reinstall the USB bus drivers

Press Win + X and open Device Manager. Expand Universal Serial Bus controllers, right-click your USB host controller or root hub, select Uninstall device, then restart your PC to force Windows to reload the drivers. This clears a stuck USB stack that's letting the mouse draw power (hence the light) but not pass movement data through.

4. Unpair and re-pair the receiver (wireless)

Unplug your 2.4GHz USB dongle, turn the mouse off for about 10 seconds, re-insert the dongle into a USB 2.0 port if you have one available, then power the mouse back on. This forces a fresh pairing handshake, which resolves most cases where a wireless mouse shows as connected but sends no movement.

FAQ

Why is my mouse light on but not working?

The light only confirms the mouse has power. Start with the surface, then the optical lens, then the USB connection — any of the three can fail independently of the LED.

Does a red or lit-up sensor mean my mouse is working?

No — it means the LED has power. The cursor still depends on the sensor tracking the surface and that data reaching your computer over USB or Bluetooth.

How do I fix a wireless mouse that won't move the cursor?

Unplug the receiver, power the mouse off for 10 seconds, move the receiver to a USB 2.0 port, then power the mouse back on to force a re-pair.
