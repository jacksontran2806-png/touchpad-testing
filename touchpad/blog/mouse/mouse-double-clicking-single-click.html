Why Is My Mouse Double-Clicking on a Single Click?

Updated September 2026 · 4 min read

A single click registering twice is almost always caused by mechanical switch wear — commonly called key chatter — or an overly sensitive Windows double-click threshold. You can usually resolve it in a few minutes by adjusting software debounce, cleaning the switch, or tweaking a couple of settings, no new mouse required.

1. Adjust the Windows double-click speed

Go to Settings → Bluetooth & devices → Mouse → Additional mouse settings. Under the Buttons tab, move the double-click speed slider slightly toward Fast. This narrows the window Windows uses to register two clicks as one double-click, which reduces (but doesn't eliminate) accidental double-triggers from a slightly worn switch.

2. Increase the software debounce time

If your mouse has vendor software — Logitech G HUB, Razer Synapse, SteelSeries GG, or a generic tool like X-Mouse Button Control — look for a Debounce Time setting and raise it to somewhere around 8ms–12ms. This tells the mouse to ignore any second signal that arrives within that window, which is exactly the accidental double-trigger a worn switch produces. It's the single most effective software fix, and it's free.

3. Blow out static or dust

Unplug or power off the mouse. Use compressed air to blow under the left and right click buttons while repeatedly pressing them to help dislodge anything trapped inside the switch housing. Dust or static buildup around the contacts occasionally causes a switch to bounce even when it isn't significantly worn — this is a five-minute check worth ruling out before assuming the switch itself is dying.

4. Update the mouse drivers

Open Device Manager, expand Mice and other pointing devices, right-click your mouse, and select Update driver. A stale or corrupted HID driver rarely causes double-clicking on its own, but it's a quick check, especially if the double-clicking started right after a Windows update.

Still double-clicking in a specific game or app?

If it only shows up in Valorant, Minecraft, or another game, the underlying cause is the same worn switch — games just have tighter input timing, so a bounce that's invisible in everyday use fires a second shot, block placement, or menu selection. Setting the debounce time in step 2 usually clears it up in-game too, since the fix happens before the signal ever reaches the game.

FAQ

Why does my mouse click twice when I click once?

A worn microswitch bounces on contact and sends two electrical signals within milliseconds of one press. It's mechanical wear, not a software bug — though software debounce can mask it.

How do I stop my mouse from double clicking without buying a new one?

Raise the debounce time in your mouse's software to 8ms–12ms (or use X-Mouse Button Control if it has none). That filters the second signal out without replacing anything.

Does the Windows double-click speed setting fix this?

Only partially — it changes how close two intentional clicks need to be to count as one double-click, not whether the switch itself misfires. A real debounce setting in your mouse software is the more reliable fix.
