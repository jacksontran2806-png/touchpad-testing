Try WASD + Shift + Space first, then add more fingers. Most keyboards top out somewhere between 2 and 6 keys unless they're specifically built for full N-key rollover.

Why this can't be fully automatic

A browser only ever finds out about a keypress when the keyboard sends one. If a key is silently dropped, no event fires — there's nothing to detect. So this tool shows you the truth honestly: how many of the keys you're physically holding actually register. You judge the gap.

What "ghosting" actually means

Most keyboards wire keys in a grid so a handful of wires can cover a hundred-plus keys. Press three keys that happen to share the same rows and columns, and the electronics can't tell them apart from a fourth key that was never pressed — that phantom fourth press is a "ghost key." Cheaper boards prevent this with a small blocklist of known-bad combos (key blocking): safer, but it means a key you're genuinely pressing just doesn't register. Full N-key rollover (NKRO) keyboards wire around the problem entirely, usually via USB with a different reporting protocol.

What to do with your result

  1. 2-6 key rollover is normal for a standard membrane keyboard — fine for typing, limiting for gaming.
  2. If WASD + a couple of modifiers doesn't reliably register together, check for an "NKRO mode" toggle — many mechanical keyboards ship in 6-key mode by default.
  3. For full rollover, look specifically for "NKRO" in the keyboard's spec sheet — it's not universal even on mechanical boards.
